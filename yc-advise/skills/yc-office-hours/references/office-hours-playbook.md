# Office-hours playbook

Read only the sections relevant to the founder's current bottleneck. These are diagnostic prompts, not a questionnaire to recite.

## Stage routing

### Idea and validation

Find out:

- Who has the problem, how often it occurs, and what they do today.
- Whether the founders have observed the problem directly or are relying on a plausible story.
- How they can reach five specific prospective users this week.

Prefer a narrow problem, direct conversations about past behavior, and a lightweight test of urgency. Do not recommend building a broad platform to discover whether the problem exists.

### MVP and first users

Find out:

- The smallest end-to-end outcome the product must deliver.
- What can be done manually behind the scenes.
- How quickly a real user can try it and how the team will watch them use it.

Cut features that do not test the core value. A useful but unpolished product in users' hands usually teaches more than an elegant internal prototype.

### Pre-product-market fit

Find out:

- Which users return without being chased, what they would miss, and why they stop.
- Whether the team reviews individual user behavior and speaks with retained and churned users.
- The single weakest step between acquisition, activation, repeated use, and payment.

Prefer cohort retention, frequency of the core action, and qualitative evidence of pull over cumulative registrations or traffic.

### Growth and post-product-market fit

Find out:

- What evidence shows durable pull rather than a temporary spike.
- Which constraint now limits growth: market reach, onboarding, reliability, sales capacity, hiring, or cash.
- What must remain true about the product as the company scales.

Do not mechanically apply pre-PMF minimalism after users are pulling the product out of the team. Address the active constraint while protecting retention and product quality.

## Business-model lenses

Use the lens that matches the company rather than forcing every startup into one scoreboard.

- **Consumer:** activation into the core action, cohort retention, frequency, organic/referral behavior, and sustainable acquisition.
- **B2B SaaS:** time to value, usage by the intended user, conversion, retention or expansion, sales-cycle learning, and deployment friction.
- **Marketplace:** successful matches or transactions, time to match, repeat behavior on both sides, liquidity by a narrow market, and take rate only after value exists.
- **Developer product:** time to first success, repeated production use, depth of integration, retained active projects, and bottoms-up spread.
- **Hard tech, biotech, or regulated products:** technical milestone risk, customer or partner validation, capital and regulatory path, and the fastest credible de-risking step. Avoid pretending that a seven-day revenue test fits every company.

## Fundraising

First determine whether fundraising is the present bottleneck or a distraction from customer progress.

Help the founder express:

- What the company does in one concrete sentence.
- The specific insight or change that makes now the right time.
- Evidence of demand, founder-market fit, rate of progress, and a credible route to a large outcome.
- How much capital is needed, what milestone it buys, and what happens if the round takes longer than expected.

Keep facts, forecasts, and aspirations distinct. Never invent traction, compress unfavorable metrics, or advise manufacturing urgency. For securities, SAFE, tax, or governance decisions, encourage qualified legal and financial advice.

## Cofounders and hiring

For cofounder tension, identify the concrete disagreement, decision owner, shared company goal, and whether the pattern is about trust, performance, equity, role clarity, or values. Encourage a direct conversation with observable commitments and a review date. Serious legal, safety, harassment, or employment issues require appropriate professional support.

Before recommending a hire, ask what bottleneck the role removes, why a founder cannot own it for now, and what success looks like in 30 to 90 days. Early teams usually benefit from people who can own outcomes across ambiguity; avoid adding management layers before the work requires them.

## Weekly experiment template

- **Claim:** What must be true?
- **Test:** What is the smallest action that could disconfirm it?
- **Audience:** Which exact users or customers will be involved?
- **Owner:** Who will do the work?
- **Signal:** What behavior or result counts as meaningful evidence?
- **Deadline:** When will the team review it?
- **Decision:** What will the team do if the signal is strong, weak, or ambiguous?
